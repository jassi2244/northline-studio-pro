Process

Simple enough to move fast.

A clear working process keeps projects moving without unnecessary agency-style ceremony, while still giving important decisions the attention they need.

Delivery flow

Clear from brief to launch.

Every project has its own requirements, but the overall flow stays deliberately simple so communication and delivery remain clear.

01 / Discover

Understand the job

Business context, audience, goals, references, required features, content, integrations and technical constraints are clarified first.

02 / Scope

Define the build

Pages, functionality, technical approach, responsibilities, dependencies, milestones and project scope are agreed before development.

03 / Build

Design and develop

Work progresses through visible milestones, allowing important interface and technical decisions to be reviewed before moving too far.

04 / Launch

Test and hand over

Final QA, responsive checks, deployment, launch support and any agreed documentation or post-launch support complete the delivery.

Working principles

Enough structure. No unnecessary process.

The process is structured where it protects quality and reduces mistakes, but kept light enough that the actual work stays the priority.

01 / Direct

Communication stays direct

Questions, reviews and technical decisions happen directly without unnecessary layers between the client and the person building.

02 / Visible

Review meaningful progress

Reviews happen around useful milestones, not constant small updates that slow the project without improving the result.

03 / Practical

Decisions stay product-focused

Design and technical decisions are judged against the business goal, usability, maintainability and delivery requirements.

Commercial model

Scope-based, not menu-priced.

Each project is quoted around the actual scope, functionality, technical requirements and delivery effort. Payment is normally structured around agreed project milestones.

The proposal defines scope, responsibilities, commercial terms, timeline assumptions and any ongoing support before work begins.

Before we start

A useful brief makes everything easier.

You do not need a perfect specification. A clear explanation of the business, problem and expected outcome is enough to begin.

Business

What does the company or product do, and who is the intended audience?

Objective

What should the website or application help the business achieve?

Requirements

What pages, features, integrations or workflows are already known?

Existing setup

Share any existing website, application, branding, hosting or technical system that the project needs to work with.

Timeline

Mention any important target date or dependency. The final schedule can then be planned realistically.

Ready to discuss?

Start with the actual problem.

Tell me what you are planning, what already exists and what the finished product should help you achieve. From there we can shape the right scope and approach.
